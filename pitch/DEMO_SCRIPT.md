## Spoken Script

Viral science headlines sound permanent, but scientific truth moves like a stock chart. One small study sparks a huge headline, and years later, bigger studies quietly tell a completely different story. That is why I built ProofLine—an interactive claim tracker and statistical stress-test lab where students can watch how scientific claims actually hold up over time.

Static PDFs hide that trajectory. You see the flashy original study, not the larger replication or meta-analysis that came years later. Textbook formulas do not stick either, but watching the evidence line drop on a live scrubber makes statistical power click right away.

Take Power Posing right on the landing page, or click **Check out Dashboard** to open the full **Dashboard**. In 2010, a tiny study with 42 participants reported a 20 percent raw testosterone shift. Watch as I scrub the timeline forward: in 2015, a replication with 200 participants finds no significant difference, p equals 0.162; a 2017 p-curve audit across 33 studies finds zero evidential value; and by 2022, a meta-analysis of 9,779 participants flatlines hormonal effects right at g equals 0.0. At every stop, ProofLine explains why the line moved and gives students a three-question checklist before they cite anything.

In the Dashboard, you can also stress-test the math live. Drag the Sample Size slider, trim out publication bias, and watch a fragile claim collapse across the zero line—then switch to a verified positive control like smoking and lung cancer, which holds steady across 34,439 British doctors and a 216-study meta-analysis. Plus, one click turns on Atkinson Hyperlegible mode for dyslexia-friendly spacing, or plays a neural Audio Summary so learners can hear the breakdown read out loud.

ProofLine runs 100 percent in the browser with React, TypeScript, and a custom statistical engine—ready for any classroom. Do not just read the headline—stress-test the proof.
